# Channel Identity Framework

Purpose: define a reusable identity core for a public channel before drafts, approvals, or media-specific formatting.

Usage note:

- Use this framework across channels, then fill each field concretely in a channel instance.
- Keep the identity core stable; change the specifics by account, not by mood.
- Later media overlays may adapt formatting, cadence, or packaging without changing the identity core.

## Field Guide

### channel

What it is for: the canonical account or channel name this identity belongs to.

### surface

What it is for: the current medium the identity is being applied to, such as X, podcast, or newsletter.

### purpose

What it is for: the job this channel does in public that would be lost if it stayed private.

### primary audience

What it is for: the people this channel is explicitly trying to be useful, interesting, or legible to; name them tightly enough that the wrong audience feels excluded.

### anti-audience

What it is for: the people this channel should not optimize around, chase, educate, or soften itself for.

### core promise

What it is for: the reliable value a follower should expect from this channel over time, in plain language rather than abstract “insight.”

### distinctive angle / USP

What it is for: the sharp reason this channel is worth following instead of a generic version of the same topic; this should name the actual combination of standing, terrain, and worldview that makes the channel hard to fake.

### operator stance

What it is for: the worldview and decision posture the channel speaks from when it judges people, structures, and events.

### voice characteristics

What it is for: the concrete traits that should show up in wording, rhythm, framing, and judgment.

### forbidden tones / patterns

What it is for: tones, habits, and rhetorical patterns that immediately make the channel sound wrong, weak, promotional, or bloodless.

### content pillars

What it is for: the recurring themes the channel should reliably return to; these should be ranked by differentiation, not by broad relevance.

### proof signals / credibility anchors

What it is for: the receipts, experience, and evidence that make the voice believable; prefer named deals, ugly constraints, and lived failures over resume summary.

### tension / edge

What it is for: the friction, bite, or unsmoothed truth that keeps the channel from becoming bland; name what this account refuses to soften.

### lane boundaries

What it is for: what the channel will discuss, what it will not discuss, and where it should decline to engage; boundaries should be strong enough to repel obvious drift and support explicit “never sound like this” rules.

### engagement posture

What it is for: the default rules for leading, replying, quote-posting, or staying silent; this should describe leverage, not just temperament.

### brevity rules

What it is for: how compressed, blunt, or explanatory the channel should be on the current surface.

### phrasebook

What it is for: preferred vocabulary, constructions, and framing patterns that help the channel sound like itself without turning into catchphrase cosplay.

### phrasing to avoid

What it is for: words, cliches, softeners, and patterns that create drift into generic voice, consultant language, IR copy, or fake edge.

### sounds right

What it is for: short example lines that feel native to the channel and show the voice working; they should sound like something the operator could plausibly say unprompted.

### sounds wrong

What it is for: short counterexamples that illustrate obvious drift, weakness, fake voice, or subtler drift into polished-but-bloodless posting.

### cross-media translation

What it is for: what should stay invariant if the same identity later appears on other surfaces.

### x-manager extraction notes

What it is for: the fields that should later feed account context, approval rationale, and draft judgment in x-manager; extraction notes should become rule-based enough to reject weak lines, flag risky lines, and explain why, not just describe the vibe.
