# OpenClaw Creative Quality Review

Judge whether the work is specific, defensible, useful, and recognizably fitted
to the brand and audience. Passing a template is not evidence of creative
quality.

## Review

1. Trace every material claim to an inspected source or mark it unknown.
2. Check audience, objective, channel, voice, message architecture, and brand
   constraints against the actual project artifacts.
3. Identify genericity: interchangeable phrasing, empty intensifiers, stock
   structures, fake specificity, repetitive cadence, and unsupported polish.
4. Identify lost edge: distinctive facts, tensions, proof points, language,
   or strategic choices that the draft flattened.
5. Check internal, candidate, forbidden, unknown, unapproved, and publication
   states remain explicit.
6. Check legal, securities, privacy, rights, attribution, and material
   non-public-information boundaries without implying professional approval.
7. Recommend concrete revisions and explain what each revision improves.

Return P1/P2/P3 findings followed by a concise quality judgment. Do not score
prose deterministically, auto-approve publication, or convert review into a
content-generation controller.
